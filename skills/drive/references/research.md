# Research mode

Research serves a decision, an explanation, or a useful artifact. Name that
outcome before collecting sources.

- Search the user's own work and prior attempts first. They reveal constraints
  and failure modes that public best-practice posts cannot.
- Make the pilot one narrow question, one disputed slice, or at most three
  representative items. Choose it for information gain, not convenience.
- Define the source or manual check that could falsify the emerging answer.
- The first deliverable should be the shortest useful synthesis or prototype,
  not a comprehensive appendix. More research is a later option, not a default.
- Propose external browsing only after the local precedent pass identifies the
  live uncertainty. When later executed, use primary sources for technical facts
  and label inference.

Good pilot evidence includes a user browsing a small argument map, a decision
maker reading a one-page memo, or a source-backed answer to the highest-impact
uncertainty. "Many pages collected" is not evidence of usefulness.
